Overlap h = 30; 20,000 replications per null setting; section 4: 1,000 MA(29) series per number of origins, 1,000 bootstrap resamples each; section 5: the first 200 of those series, 1,000 Gaussian draws each per taper; seed 20261016; Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.5.4, SciPy 1.18.1, pandas 3.0.6. Monte Carlo standard errors (MC SE) are binomial. A size-corrected p-value is the share of simulated null series whose pre-registered p-value (the larger of the HLN and Bartlett 60 p-values) is at most the committed one.

## What this shows

- Under the MA(29) null, with the first 20,000 draws of `dm_size_study.py` (seed 20261016): the selection 0.069, H1 0.095, H2 0.022, and H2 0.045 after Holm. These are the erratum's values.
- Mean autocorrelation over lags 30-120 (in brackets: the share of MA(29) series of the same length that reach it): the selection -0.118 (0.779), below the MA(29) mean; H1 0.126 (0.015), more than MA(29) produces at the 5 % level; H2 0.042 (0.080), above the MA(29) mean but within its range.
- At a nominal 5 % the pre-registered rule rejects at most 9.1 % of the MA(29) series; under the nulls built from the observed series it rejects from 1.7 % (the selection, Gaussian, Bartlett taper 120) to 17.0 % (H2, block bootstrap, 90 days).
- H1: 0.094-0.164 under the 8 nulls built from the observed series (0 below 0.05; MA(29) 0.095): not significant under any of them.
- H2: 0.018-0.077 under the 8 nulls built from the observed series (7 below 0.05; 0.04805 and 0.04995 within 2 MC SE of 0.05). After Holm (MC SE at most 0.004): 0.045 under MA(29); 0.05020 under the block bootstrap with blocks of 30 days and 0.036 under the Gaussian null with taper 29, the nulls built from the observed series with dependence up to about a month; and 0.068-0.155 under the 6 nulls with dependence of 45 days or more (0 below 0.05). Its significance after the size correction and Holm therefore holds only if the differential has no material dependence beyond about a month, and even then not clearly under every such null: it is below 0.05 under MA(29) and the Gaussian null with taper 29, and within 2 MC SE of 0.05 under the block bootstrap with blocks of 30 days.
- The selection: 0.083-0.130 under the block bootstrap nulls and 0.014-0.068 under the Gaussian nulls. A correctly sized gate fails under MA(29) (0.069) and under 5 of the 8 nulls built from the observed series (0.068-0.130), and passes under the Gaussian nulls with tapers 60, 90 and 120 (0.014-0.049; 0.04860 within 2 MC SE of 0.05). Under these the rule rejects only 1.7-5.4 % of the null series: they carry the development differential's negative autocorrelation beyond lag 29 (section 1) into the null.
- When MA(29) is true the block bootstrap rejects 3.9-6.8 % of the series with 486 origins and 6.3-8.5 % of the series with 336 origins at a nominal 5 %; none is more than 2 MC SE below 5 %, and 3 of the 8 are more than 2 MC SE above it (blocks of 90 days with 486 origins, blocks of 60 and 90 days with 336 origins), so where the method errs it rejects too often, and its p-values are, if anything, too small. For MA(29) series with the committed statistic its median p-value is 0.060-0.073 for the selection (exact 0.069), 0.078-0.098 for H1 (exact 0.095), 0.020-0.027 for H2 (exact 0.022), at most 0.005 above the exact value: when MA(29) holds the method does not raise p-values materially. A single series' bootstrap p-value varies, though: the share of MA(29) series whose bootstrap p-value reaches the observed one is 0.001-0.281 for the selection, 0.100-0.312 for H1, 0.061-0.495 for H2.
- Under the block bootstrap with blocks of 30, 45 and 60 days the selection's bootstrap p-values (0.115-0.130) are larger than the bootstrap gives MA(29) series with the same statistic, although its dependence beyond lag 29 is below the MA(29) mean (section 1). The block bootstrap also keeps the marginal distribution, which is skewed for the selection (-2.28) and H2 (-2.09), while the MA(29) and Gaussian nulls are symmetric; this study does not separate that from the other explanations (the shape of the dependence within a month, level shifts).
- When MA(29) is true the Gaussian nulls reject 5.5-9.5 % of the series with 486 origins and 5.5-11.0 % of the series with 336 origins at a nominal 5 % (MC SE at most 2.2 points), more the longer the taper. For MA(29) series with the committed statistic their median p-value is 0.049-0.071 for the selection (exact 0.069), 0.057-0.092 for H1 (exact 0.095), 0.009-0.021 for H2 (exact 0.022), falling as the taper grows: with long tapers the method gives p-values below the exact ones when MA(29) holds, as the downward bias of the demeaned sample autocovariance at long lags (section 1), which these nulls take as the truth, leads one to expect.
- Under the Gaussian nulls with tapers 60, 90 and 120, where the selection gate passes (0.014-0.049), 0.155-0.220 of the MA(29) series with the committed statistic get a p-value at or below the selection's: a pass under these nulls is not unusual when MA(29) holds, and under MA(29) a correctly sized gate fails (0.069).
- With tapers 60, 90 and 120 the Gaussian p-values of H2 (0.034-0.050) and H1 (0.123-0.164) are larger than the method gives most MA(29) series with the committed statistic: 0.035-0.065 of them reach H2's and 0.035-0.060 H1's, while with taper 29 both are within the range MA(29) series give. As the method lowers p-values when MA(29) holds, it is the confirmation differentials' own autocovariance beyond lag 29 that raises them.

## Limitations

- The analyses are post hoc: the nulls and the long-lag statistic were chosen after the M2 results and a first look at the autocorrelations were known. They do not replace the pre-registered decision, and the data cannot show which null is right.
- The Gaussian nulls take the demeaned sample autocovariance as the truth. It is noisy and biased downward at long lags (section 1: under MA(29) the mean autocorrelation over lags 30-120 averages -0.054 with 486 origins and -0.072 with 336 origins), and the Bartlett taper shrinks it further, so a null with a long taper carries that bias and has no dependence beyond its taper. Section 5 measures the effect when MA(29) is true.
- The circular block bootstrap keeps dependence only within a block, weakened at the joins; a resample of 336 origins holds only 4 blocks of 90 days. It keeps the skewed marginal distribution of the selection and H2 differentials (section 0), which the MA(29) and Gaussian nulls do not; the study does not separate the effect of the skewness from that of the dependence.
- Every size-corrected p-value carries Monte Carlo error (section 2): a value within 2 MC SE of 0.05 could fall on the other side of it with another seed, and the ends of a range are its least precise values. A bootstrap or Gaussian p-value of a single series of 336-486 overlapping observations is itself noisy: sections 4 and 5 show how much it varies when MA(29) is true.
- None of the nulls models level shifts or other non-stationarity.
- M3 uses the same rule on the same grid; this study does not cover its tests.
